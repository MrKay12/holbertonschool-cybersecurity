0. PURPOSE, SCOPE AND DEFINITIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

0.1 Purpose
  A recent penetration test showed that three accounts on the production
  bastion could be taken over with an automated password-guessing tool in
  under 20 minutes. This policy sets the minimum identity and authentication
  controls that prevent a stolen or guessed password from being enough to
  access NovaTech systems.

0.2 Scope
  - All Linux servers operated by NovaTech, starting with the DMZ bastion
    novatech-prod-03, and every account on them: employees, contractors,
    administrators and service (non-human) accounts.
  - Exceptions are allowed only through section 8.

0.3 Wording
  "SHALL" / "SHALL NOT" = mandatory.  "SHOULD" = expected unless a
  documented reason exists.

0.4 Roles
  CISO                 owns this policy, approves exceptions.
  System administrators  implement and monitor the controls.
  Managers             approve access requests and access reviews.
  HR                   notifies joiners, movers and leavers.
  Users                protect their credentials and devices, report loss.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PASSWORD POLICY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Passwords remain in use for local console login, sudo and password
changes. They SHALL NOT be accepted for SSH (section 3).

1.1 Complexity (enforced by pam_pwquality)
  Every new password SHALL:
  - be at least 12 characters long                       (minlen = 12)
  - contain at least 1 uppercase, 1 lowercase, 1 digit and 1 special
    character                       (ucredit/lcredit/dcredit/ocredit = -1)
  - not contain the username                              (usercheck = 1)
  - differ from the previous password by at least 3 characters (difok = 3)
  - not be a dictionary word or a known weak password (dictionary check)
  Users SHOULD prefer long passphrases (4+ unrelated words), in line with
  NIST SP 800-63B, which values length over character variety.

1.2 History (enforced by pam_pwhistory)
  - The last 5 passwords SHALL NOT be reused             (remember = 5)
  - Old password hashes are stored root-only in /etc/security/opasswd.

1.3 Aging (enforced by /etc/login.defs and chage)
  - Maximum age: 90 days                              (PASS_MAX_DAYS 90)
  - Minimum age: 1 day, to stop cycling through the
    history in one sitting                             (PASS_MIN_DAYS 1)
  - Expiry warning: 14 days before                    (PASS_WARN_AGE 14)
  Note: NIST SP 800-63B advises against routine rotation. The 90-day rule
  is kept because NovaTech's compliance frameworks require it. It will be
  reconsidered when those frameworks allow.

1.4 Compromised passwords
  - A password known or suspected to be compromised (pentest finding,
    breach list, phishing) SHALL be changed at next login: the password is
    expired immediately (passwd -e).
  - Passwords SHALL NOT be shared, written down unprotected, or sent by
    email or chat.

1.5 Storage
  Passwords SHALL be stored only as salted, slow hashes (yescrypt or
  SHA-512 crypt). Plain text or reversible storage is forbidden.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. ACCOUNT LOCKOUT POLICY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Per-account lockout (enforced by pam_faillock)
  - An account SHALL be locked after 5 failed authentication attempts
    within 15 minutes                       (deny = 5, fail_interval = 900)
  - The lock lasts 15 minutes, then is lifted automatically
                                                        (unlock_time = 900)
  - root is not locked by pam_faillock, so the console remains an
    emergency path. Network login as root is forbidden (section 3).

2.2 Per-source banning (enforced by fail2ban)
  - A source IP with 5 failed SSH logins within 10 minutes SHALL be
    banned for 1 hour   (maxretry = 5, findtime = 600, bantime = 3600)
  - Administrators' management addresses MAY be whitelisted (ignoreip)
    with CISO approval.

2.3 Unlocking
  - Early unlock or unban SHALL be done by an administrator after
    confirming the user's identity, and logged in a ticket.
  - Repeated lockouts of one account or many accounts from one source
    SHALL be investigated as a possible attack.

2.4 Rationale
  Lockout and banning are complementary: lockout protects each account
  against guessing; banning stops one source from trying many accounts.
  Thresholds are moderate on purpose, so attackers cannot lock out real
  users at will (denial of service).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SSH ACCESS POLICY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Authentication
  - SSH password authentication SHALL be disabled
                                          (PasswordAuthentication no)
  - SSH SHALL require public-key authentication AND a second factor
    (section 4)    (AuthenticationMethods publickey,keyboard-interactive)
  - Direct root login over SSH SHALL be disabled.

3.2 Keys
  - Keys SHALL be ED25519 (preferred) or RSA of at least 3072 bits.
  - Private keys SHALL be protected by a passphrase, stay on the owner's
    device, and SHALL NOT be shared or copied to servers.
  - Private key files SHALL be mode 600, ~/.ssh mode 700,
    authorized_keys mode 600 and owned by the user.
  - Each person SHALL have their own key. Shared keys are forbidden.
  - Public keys are deployed only through an approved request
    (section 5.1). Unknown keys found in authorized_keys SHALL be removed
    and investigated.

3.3 Key review and rotation
  - authorized_keys files SHALL be reviewed quarterly with the access
    review (section 5.3).
  - Keys SHALL be rotated at least every 2 years, and immediately when a
    device is lost, stolen or decommissioned.

3.4 Session security
  - Logins SHALL be logged (source IP, user, method) and logs kept for at
    least 1 year.
  - A legal warning banner SHALL be displayed before login.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. MFA REQUIREMENTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Requirement
  - All interactive SSH access to production servers SHALL use two
    factors: the user's SSH private key (something you have: laptop) and
    a TOTP code (something you have: phone / authenticator app).
  - Possession of a stolen key alone, or a stolen TOTP device alone, SHALL
    NOT be enough to log in.

4.2 TOTP settings (pam_google_authenticator)
  - Time-based codes (RFC 6238), 30-second step
  - A code SHALL NOT be accepted twice                (disallow reuse)
  - At most 3 login attempts per 30 seconds           (rate limit)
  - Window of 3 codes to tolerate clock skew          (window size 3)
  - 5 single-use emergency scratch codes per user

4.3 Enrollment
  - New users SHALL enroll before their SSH access is activated.
  - The secret / QR code SHALL be shown only to the user (in person or over
    an encrypted screen share), never sent by email or chat.
  - Secret files (~/.google_authenticator) SHALL be readable only by
    their owner (mode 400 or 600).
  - Scratch codes SHALL be stored offline (sealed paper or password
    manager), never on the same device as the authenticator app.

4.4 Rollout and exceptions
  - During the transition, users without a TOTP secret MAY be allowed to
    log in with their key only (nullok) for at most 30 days, as tracked
    by Security Engineering.
  - Non-interactive service accounts are covered by section 5.5.

4.5 Verification
  - MFA enforcement SHALL be tested after every change to SSH or PAM, and
    at least quarterly (19-test_mfa.sh).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ACCOUNT LIFECYCLE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Creation (joiner)
  - Every account SHALL be requested in a ticket by the user's manager,
    stating the role, the systems and the privileges needed.
  - Each person SHALL have a unique, named account. Shared or generic
    human accounts are forbidden.
  - Least privilege: sudo rights only for approved administrators, through
    a named group, never by adding users directly to sudoers.
  - The initial password SHALL be random, delivered through a separate
    channel, and expired so it must be changed at first login.
  - SSH key deployment and TOTP enrollment (section 4.3) SHALL be
    completed before SSH access is granted.
  - Aging defaults (section 1.3) apply automatically to new accounts.

5.2 Modification (mover)
  - Any privilege change (e.g. sudo access) SHALL be requested and
    approved like a creation.
  - When a user changes role, rights no longer needed SHALL be removed
    within 5 working days.

5.3 Periodic review
  - Every quarter, managers SHALL confirm each of their team's accounts
    and privileges, and administrators SHALL review sudo membership and
    authorized_keys files.
  - Accounts unused for 90 days SHALL be disabled, then removed after a
    further 90 days unless the manager justifies them.

5.4 Termination (leaver)
  HR SHALL notify IT on or before the last working day. The same day,
  administrators SHALL:
  - lock the account and expire it  (usermod -L, chage -E 0)
  - remove its SSH keys, TOTP secret and sudo/group memberships
  - end its active sessions and processes
  - review its cron jobs and files, and transfer ownership as needed
  For an involuntary termination, these steps SHALL be done before the
  person is told. The account (home directory archived) SHALL be deleted
  after 90 days.

5.5 Service (non-human) accounts
  - SHALL have a named human owner and a documented purpose.
  - SHALL NOT have an interactive shell (nologin) unless SSH access is
    required. In that case: key-only, restricted in authorized_keys to the
    source address and command needed (from=, command=, no-pty).
  - SHALL NOT have a usable password, and are exempt from password aging.
  - TOTP exemptions for service accounts SHALL be documented exceptions
    (section 8) and reviewed quarterly.

6. EMERGENCY ACCESS PROCEDURES (SUMMARY)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Full procedure: 20-emergency_procedures.txt. Key rules:

6.1 Principles
  - MFA SHALL NEVER be disabled globally. Bypasses are per user, limited
    to 24 hours, and still require the SSH key.
  - Identity SHALL be verified out-of-band before any change (callback on
    the directory number, manager approval). Urgency does not waive it.
  - Two people: one approves, another performs the change.

6.2 Situations covered
  - Lost phone / TOTP device: revoke the secret, use a scratch code or a
    time-limited bypass, re-enroll on a new device.
  - Lost laptop / SSH key: remove the key from authorized_keys, deploy a
    new one.
  - Lockout after failed attempts: unlock (faillock) or unban (fail2ban).
  - No administrator can log in: another administrator, then the
    hypervisor console, then configuration backups, then recovery mode.
  - Break-glass account: credentials sealed in the safe (two-person
    access); every use is an incident followed by credential rotation.

6.3 Audit trail
  Every emergency access SHALL have a ticket (requester, approver,
  performer, verification, reason, times), be logged, be closed within
  24 hours (bypass removed, controls re-tested, exposed credentials
  rotated) and be reviewed by the security team.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. MONITORING AND COMPLIANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Controls SHALL be audited after any change and at least quarterly.
    Evidence: 1-pam_audit.sh (configuration audit), 3-test_complexity.sh,
    4-password_history.sh, 8-configure_faillock.sh (lockout test),
    11-fail2ban_manage.sh status, 19-test_mfa.sh.
  - Authentication logs (SSH, sudo, faillock, fail2ban) SHALL be kept for
    at least 1 year and protected from modification.
  - Configuration backups SHALL be taken before every change to PAM or
    SSH (.bak / .backup files) to allow rollback.
  - Violations of this policy SHALL be reported to the CISO and may lead to
    access removal and disciplinary action.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. EXCEPTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Any deviation from a SHALL requirement needs a written exception
  approved by the CISO. Each exception records the system, accounts,
  reason, compensating controls and an expiry date (maximum 12 months).
  Exceptions are reviewed at every policy review.
